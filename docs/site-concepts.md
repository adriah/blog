# sigkill.no — directions for a personal site

Saved 2026-09-11. Selected direction: **1. The last signal**.

## Intent

Make sigkill.no a personal landmark with an archive attached: a site that feels complete even if nothing is published for two years. Occasional short notes and substantial technical guides should both belong here.

Take inspiration from the polish of the local gh0st.no project: a metallic orbital symbol, huge typography, darkness, negative space, and restrained motion. Give sigkill.no its own character through interruption, control, and the moment a signal disappears.

## 1. The last signal — selected

An almost black screen. A thin luminous waveform travels across it, passes behind a large sigkill.no wordmark, and abruptly becomes a flat line. A faint afterimage hangs in the darkness. Bone-white type with one sharp red accent.

Suggested line: “Some things are better terminated.”

Three understated links lead to Writing (labelled Notes), Projects, and Elsewhere. The writing section inherits the waveform as a dividing line, oversized article titles, and beautifully styled code blocks.

Why it works: directly tied to the domain, visually memorable, and a natural sibling to gh0st.no. SIGKILL is signal 9 and cannot be caught, blocked, or ignored. Use that sparingly as supporting detail; do not imply SIGKILL generates a core dump.

## 2. The kill switch

One beautifully rendered physical object: a recessed industrial switch in dark brushed metal, with a tiny red status light. Large, precise domain typography surrounds it.

Pressing the switch kills the ambient animation and lights. The page remains usable in a stark emergency-light state. A second press resets the scene.

Writing lives in “Field notes”: technical articles with instrument-panel details, crisp diagrams, and small warning-red annotations. The OpenBSD router article is a natural fit.

Why it works: the most tactile and playful direction. The central object needs excellent execution.

## 3. After the process

A suspended sculpture made of fine silver threads or particles, caught halfway through disintegrating. Slow movement, enormous negative space, and a cold, abstract atmosphere.

Suggested line: “The process ends. The ideas remain.”

The archive becomes “Remnants”: notes, experiments, longer essays, and abandoned projects worth keeping. Expressive article titles and abstract artwork surround quiet, readable text.

Why it works: closest to gh0st.no's atmosphere, with a personal and contemplative character.

## 4. The independent machine

A small, meticulously designed fictional Unix workstation interface. Graphite, amber highlights, process identifiers, and subtle grid lines. Enough detail to suggest a working machine, with ordinary links for immediate navigation.

Suggested line: “Locally administered. Occasionally updated.”

“Notes / Builds / About” lead into a handsome system journal. Old-web personality comes through a small colophon, RSS, links to other people's sites, and occasional hidden details.

Why it works: fits the independence and dislike of web bloat expressed in the original homepage.

## Shared direction for the writing

Use “Notes” as the main label. Give the archive and articles the same attention as the entrance: big editorial titles, excellent typography, generous spacing, distinctive code blocks, and a shared accent and signal motif. Motion settles down when someone reads.

Keep the existing /blog/ paths and article URLs. Retain the existing Markdown writing workflow, tags, RSS, source links, and contact details. Avoid inventing projects or setting an expectation of regular publishing.

The original recommendation was The last signal with some of The independent machine's personality: a dramatic entrance and a useful little corner of the internet behind it.
